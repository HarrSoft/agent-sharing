# **Theoretical preface** {#theoretical-preface}

*(if you get bored, skip it.)*

Within Moneyless Society, we recognize that positive and sustainable change happens not in isolation, but as a consequence of coordinated direct action. Corporations, for profit or not, tend to revolve around offering specific products or services that fulfill their organizational mission. The members may share a general alignment that is reflected in some public statement on their website, but there is a clear distinction between what the organization’s concerns are and what the rest of the world needs to do. There are clear patterns to what kinds of experts are employed or volunteering.  
To explain what defines us and sets our group apart, we must first shift our focus from the granularity of products and services to the historical context of societal institutions. We want to live in a world where social institutions are organized around different *principles*, namely:  
expanding compassion,  
systemic efficiency,  
ecological regeneration,  
science,  
autonomy,   
respect,  
diversity,  
wellbeing, and   
joy.  
There are a few ways to describe what that society looks like—competing ideas of how its institutions would be shaped. We hold close to heart the belief that justice is so strongly desired around the globe in almost every human’s heart, that society would function better if it was more consistent with that sense of justice. Today, our rights are heinously violated both by overt acts of violence towards our persons, and by the systemic control of our access to the means of survival and thriving. The system that gates our access was built with more than one mechanism, and it has perpetuated itself by reinforcing people’s belief in certain core social contracts that permeate our daily lived experience as deeply as the air we breathe.

## Social Contracts {#social-contracts}

What the hell is a social contract? Are we calling them all out or just certain ones? Well, a social contract is a shared understanding of how people ought to interact with one another. This is our living list of some common social contracts that we reject, whether they are explicit or implicit:  
👎“Value can be objectively quantified.”👎 Any one thing will have different value to everyone who looks at it.  
👎“Goods and services should move between people via exchanges of equal value. That’s fair.”👎 There are many possible economies that don’t have trade. We gravitate towards gifting, and when we can, we give what we can to each other if it is helpful, and we know that we will probably but not necessarily get help from them in return later. All people give unquantifiable help and support to each other all the time, as social beings, and enjoy doing so. Trading things of equal value is not an efficient way of making sure the most people have what they need, or to sustain our species’ lifespan, or even for people to have the experiences that would be most fulfilling to them. It encourages people to compete rather than collaborate towards an optimal outcome for all. Trade is just patriarchal, toxic, primate aggression distilled into a game that’s impossible for most people to quit.  
👎“People need to earn their survival.”👎 The world would be a better place for everybody if they didn’t have to worry about useless work, if activities of their daily living could be automated or simply done by somebody else who wants to do it voluntarily. Voluntary, cybernetic, crowd-planned, direct action could provide everything people need today *and* the kinds of things people want, but more sustainably, ethically, scientifically, and enjoyably. People would still have more free time overall with such an economic shift. The goal should be to reduce the amount of work we all have to do, not force people to endure decades of drudgery under countless passive threats. When living essentials cost money, and most of us need to work for somebody richer to receive money, this means most of us are being told by the wealthy “do what we say or starve”. To “earn” implies that you are being rewarded for doing something good. Is helping Jeff Bezos to buy another mansion really the kind of thing society should reward?  
👎“Everyone deserves the amount of money they have, and having a lot of money means you’re successful”👎. How much money you have is almost entirely a function of how much privilege you had growing up. More good would come from living in a society based on equality, driven to give everybody as many opportunities as possible.

With regard to the injustice of people being denied the freedom to live how they want, we see this situation for what it is: wealthy bloodlines and nouveau riche alike have, over centuries, deteriorated the commons and subsequently established private monopolies over the means of survival. They have supported this theft from the public through violent, unsustainable colonialism. The lack of sustainability for this system does not mean that a better world will fall into our laps, but that a *more sustainable* system can overtake it. Being more sustainable means fewer internal contradictions. The destination of this train of thought is as follows: Moneyless Society aims to undermine the monopoly over living essentials by amplifying efforts to build commons that can provision resources so abundantly that people would have better lives if they quit the money economy. We are evolving as a species, leaving old systems for better functioning ones.  
One day in the future does not hold more value than today. Laser focus on political goals can blind people to the harms of the means they use to win battles, poisoning their ability to think creatively about how they themselves incorporate compassionate values into the daily patterns they follow. It is our shared belief that the power of habits to shape people’s worldviews, in conjunction with the invisibility-from-ubiquity of the social contracts that underlie their daily interactions with other people, is a powerful force of stagnation hindering transformative social change.   
Therefore, Moneyless Society is committed to means that are in alignment with our values. We will fail, over and over, because we have not even perceived the ways that our default habits and unquestioned social contracts fail all of us. Acknowledging our failure creates an opportunity to invent alternative modes of being, even if sometimes they must be deployed at a larger scale than the individual is capable of. One of the greatest inconsistencies we will face, on a continual basis, is our members’ need to participate in the money economy to whatever extent is necessary for them to have a happy life. Even those of us providing living essentials will have to charge money for it—yet, by awakening the cognitive dissonance between our principles and our actions, we make ourselves yearn for the system we want to build.

# **What are the concerned people to do?** {#what-are-the-concerned-people-to-do?}

## Gift Renaissance {#gift-renaissance}

There are a lot of concerned people, although many of those people do not feel like that’s the case. For those in the workforce, depending on the workplace (where they spend the most time around other people), there is not much opportunity to find people who share their frustrations with the world, because many of those frustrations are considered heretical. We feel an alienation because our environment makes us feel that way, and that alienation prevents us from even beginning to work on the problems. The alienation makes us fear other people, and avoid collaborating. Even if cool cybernetic systems existed for communities to collectively allocate resources efficiently, they would fall flat on a person who has never received true kindness.  
This means our action begins with personal healing processes and simple acts of benevolence that restore our faith in each other. When a need is expressed, we try to help. When a need is perceived, rather than expressed, it may be appropriate to offer help, but we should avoid the savior mentality where we pressure somebody in need into becoming dependent on us. Good help is defined by the person who needs it, not by the people with the privilege to offer it.  
Learning the mindset of giving is the preface to really effective activism, because many forms of action that improve the world are, in essence, gifts of time or resources. Being comfortable with gifting is vital, and if you aren’t comfortable doing it, then you either need to practice or ask for help yourself to arrive at a safer, healthier, happier place in life. Somebody without capital may be able to gift their time, but they need that time (and energy) to donate in the first place.

## Cybernetic Gift Economy {#cybernetic-gift-economy}

Obviously, we cannot instantly automate all processes of production today, even if it were compatible with today’s economic model. We haven’t invented a robot to do *everything* yet. But the partner of automation is streamlining, which we can do with computer programming. In a simple, freely hosted web application, it is possible to integrate data from around the world, connect people who might never have met, and calculate answers in a more coherent and useful format than whatever you find on the first page of search results on Google.  
Sharebay.org and Buy Nothing Facebook groups are both proof that there’s much to gain from applying the potential of computers to the problem of how we organize the distribution of everything that everybody needs to everywhere. On Sharebay, people offer to give things for free, or request things they’d like to get. It works\! People do both. If you want to participate in a moneyless society, you can absolutely start. Sharebay also has a partner website, Honor Pay, which is a way of formalizing appreciation and letting people build up a reputation for being well respected in the community. This could appeal to people who want to be liked by people in their community (i.e. humans).  
You will not find everything you need on these platforms—this is an impetus for people to learn how to make those things. You may wish the software was more powerful—this is an opportunity for you to support the people creating open source software to expand on the existing gift infrastructure, and to express what features would make the site more appealing or accessible. Gift economy is exponentially appealing, because as more people benefit from it more often, they feel a greater sense of appreciation for the community, and want to give more back *to the community*. The perks draw people in, but realizing how much better it can be than capitalism can really make people passionate about RBE.

## Low-Resource Commodity Stopgaps {#low-resource-commodity-stopgaps}

In the immediate future, one way to eke out some working-class sovereignty from an exploitative economy is to learn how to make things that people most desperately need and directly give those things to them on our own time, in our own spaces. Because anticapitalist movements are generally made up of people who are disenfranchised (though to be fair, most people are somehow disenfranchised), there is a huge shortage in what we have access to in the first place, so it’s difficult for the movement’s participants to actually be doing the giving. Yes, in the long run, what is sought is a release of the monopoly over raw resources, land, factories, labor, and commodities, but we need an effective and easy system to distribute those things in the first place before we have any hope of ex-capitalists donating them.  
We can practice this today, by personally developing skills that we can use for the benefit of others. Whether it’s watching sewing tutorials to repair your friends ripped jackets, or reading Heater Bloc’s guide to making simple, portable heaters for unhoused people, one of the major personal commitments that many of us need to make is to increase our list of practical skills. Ideally, we would have a central list of what people need, sorted by how great the need is, so that the more utilitarian and adaptable of us could learn the skills that could help address those stated needs.

## Set Up Legally Compliant Communities {#set-up-legally-compliant-communities}

Many of the ways that we will directly participate in changing the world involve departing from standard modes of operation. Many communities have been tempted to be maximally efficient and beneficent to the environment, but most governments have hilariously bad laws in place to prevent at least some regenerative practices. Historically, many stupid laws have been written to enable the imprisonment/enslavement of radical change-makers. In other cases, there are laws preventing certain practices that seem common sense to allow, but are in place for ecological or other big-picture reasons. In either case, anybody forming physical communities or developing locations should be aware that there are folks eager to catch you slipping up so you can be prosecuted, your reputation tarnished, your message lost, so it’s vital to know the laws that could disrupt the ways you try to nurture kindness or improve your community.  
Before venturing to build neighborhoods or buy a block of houses, focus on the relationship side of community. Physical proximity makes those relationships way more humanly meaningful and fulfilling, but even relationships restrained to correspondence and video calls can constitute material camaraderie. Also, diffuse movements are harder for fascists to stamp out. Establish relationships with people who can do things you need done, and who you can do things for, and do those things for each other without “keeping track”.   
Some people have more needs, and might need more help than you do. At the same time, pay attention to when your giving is exhausting or otherwise becoming unpleasant, and be okay with focusing on yourself when you have needs. Humans are happier in relationships where they offer and accept, rather than impose and demand. Somebody with a lot of needs needs a lot of people to help. The point of society is to ensure the survival, thriving, and evolution of its members so our world can do the same, so we all ought to step up to the responsibility. Diffusion of responsibility must be reversed, and instead we should see waves of support as soon as somebody is wronged or in need.

## Acquiring Means of Production {#acquiring-means-of-production}

To level up economic independence, those communities need to get means of production. To ensure they remain in the commons and get maintained, neighbors or comrades in a trade will need to establish and write bylaws for legally compliant joint ownership frameworks, such as worker cooperatives, to:

* purchase industrial scale means of production and batches of materials than they could individually  
* buy land or buildings, fund construction, and/or rent building space  
* ensure the longevity of shared tools  
* design and maintain beautiful environments that draw people into community service by being fun  
* assume legal liability and overhead for community-provided social services, as is relevant  
* maximize access to the labor that provides for people’s wants and needs and work towards peak efficiency and automation of that labor  
* minimize the transactional nature from items changing hands  
* schedule educational programs for people of all ages in the community to learn skills that are relevant to the community’s, and the world’s, needs  
* establish social governance structures that promote equality, consent, and informed decision making by those who actually do the work

That jointly controlled entity should have a bank account, where people in the community contribute what they can.

## Condense and organize information {#condense-and-organize-information}

Teaching people how to contribute to the commons, and making that process more fun, engaging, and rewarding is not something that every community needs to do on their own. Learning how to make universally consumed items, a cup for example, is not something that every neighborhood in every county in every state/province in every country needs to accomplish. Those interested in making a moneyless world happen can vastly accelerate this process by utilizing platforms that (a) crowdsource innovation \*AND\* (b) provide that information at zero cost to the general public—or, in cases of dangerous knowhow, to any people who pass proper training and precautionary measures. “Utilizing” here would entail not just complimenting the developer, retweeting, and subscribing to their Patreon, but 

1. Adding relevant information that could illuminate a problem people face or empower them to solve it.   
2. Redirect theredirecting time you spend on draining activities towards prosocial platforms.  
3. Finding physical-world actions you can take to address the problems that you’re most passionate about.

The quality of the organization of humanity’s knowledge is lacking. Figuring out how to better organize it will never end, but as of 2023 it is not easy for activists to figure out the steps to dismantling capitalism. They don’t know what they can do with their skillset. They don’t know anybody doing the same things. Volunteer work can have barriers to entry or otherwise perpetuate certain marginalizing, power-centralizing social patterns, like maybe the person who speaks the most confidently becomes the leader in a protest. It would be great if whatever organizational framework you use for data and knowledge takes frustrations like these into account, because whether somebody ends up finding the information they need is essentially a question of how easy it is for them to find said information.

## Scale ↑ Up {#scale-↑-up}

Once the gift-economy supporters in your community (physical or local) have established a joint-ownership entity, are confident that it’s set up soundly, and start managing larger amounts of resources, think about building relationships with other communities, or even helping other communities take off in the way you did.   
For everything humanity does, there are humans who know to do it. As your gift economy welcomes more experts into the fold, it will become feasible to start operating larger machinery for mass production or other large-scale endeavors—broadly, things that take more people to pull off. Factories will be on the table, as will large scale carbon capture programs or power plants.  
When your community starts managing larger-scale, collectively-governed enterprises, it’s important that you think stochastically. Look up what “stochastic” means long before you start thinking on an industrial scale. When you implement a policy or distribute a product over a large scale, random things that *can* happen *will* happen, and it’s when you’re dealing with greater numbers that trends and rates in data start to become clear. Scaling up any endeavor will illuminate the problems within it, where subtle imperfections may make a huge difference.   
People can’t simply walk down the street to tell you your cool new electric toothbrush shocked them if you are shipping those toothbrushes from 5 states away. Most of the time, then, people who are harmed by product malfunctions or safety issues don’t do anything about it. Maybe a person will be pleasantly surprised when they get a class-action lawsuit check in the mail for $30 out of the $300 they were scammed out of, but it takes a large number of concerted organizing for those people to get their problems addressed, and frankly, if an industrial producer is making things not for profit but rather for gifts, then there’s no incentive to cover up product dangers or prevent people from speaking up.  
Small percentages can trick us into thinking that we’re dealing with small numbers. As your denominator becomes bigger, that illusion becomes falser. Saying “0.01% of the world population” feels like a smaller number of people than 800,000, even though they’re equal.  
All that said, when enterprises in a gift economy scale up, it will be vital for them to create easy ways for people to complain or warn about dangers, and seek out the holes in their design to engineer better, ever more regenerative products.

## Establish Grassroots Process for Selecting Candidates {#establish-grassroots-process-for-selecting-candidates}

If any greater transition is to happen towards a further stage of political evolution, then it will be far more feasible with at least a standing government that makes and rewrites laws to match what citizens actually care about. There is currently no way for the citizenry to elicit an ideal leader. Instead, we just let one of the rich traipse into office. Getting onto the ballot is a steep hill of awareness building—except for the rich, who can buy airtime and ads that make far more people aware of them than grassroots candidates that could make far better leaders. In an age of gross political alienation, repetition is often all that’s needed to win people over, and money lets you repeat a message more times.  
One core reason that third parties fail is that they do not represent a conceptual or systematic difference from establishment political parties, but niche ideological platforms. There is nothing grassroots, scientific, or bottom-up about existing selection of candidates within political third parties, even those that represent the interests of the lower class. For a true challenge to the system to be made, an entirely separate system must be created for selecting candidates who represent the most interests of the most people. Success of such a platform in local elections could go viral and instigate widespreadwidely spread use of it at progressively larger scales, all the way to federal congress and the presidency.

## Push Electoral Process Reform {#push-electoral-process-reform}

The grassroots elicitation of leaders, as well as their viability, could be amplified greatly by increasing activist efforts towards voting reform. The barbaric logic of our existing voting system, First Past The Post, is such that an eternal war between two parties is inevitable. People’s votes are wasted if their preferred candidate isn’t popular, so they all vote for one that’s most likely to win—one of the persons whose advocates bought the most airtime. Imagine a person who, if they were able to be president, would hands down be the country’s favorite president of all time, but they were poor. Without a change to the *process* of elections, there’s no chance they could beat the rich people who take out bountiful ads on numerous channels in every state every day. Absolutely no contest. To expect such a person to win would be asking tens of millions of people to make an implausible leap of faith, to gamble that somebody barely exposed to the public is a strong contender.   
Instead, we should be able to decide what voting system we want to use, and the people who lead us should represent as many of the people’s interests as possible. There is more than one voting system that’s fairer and less gameable, and FairVote is a great one-stop resource for all your voting system needs. Electoral process is less heady than you have been trained to feel it is, at least when FairVote explains it. Educating working class people about this kind of thing can be difficult for many advocates to broach, but any attempt that you do make to tactfully spread awareness about alternatives to our corrupt system is worth it. Planting a seed that there might be other options might take a while to sprout into wanting to understand what other options are out there, and even longer for them to be ready to jump on board. Other times, you’ll instantly flip a switch in people’s heads and get them to realize how bad a job we’re doing choosing our leaders. A late stage evolution of this awareness is sometimes an understanding that all kyriarchy is bad, but getting everybody to understand arcane political/game theory is a ludicrous taskask when achieving reform is more about getting people to agree that voting isn’t as fair as it could be.

## Organize Political Campaigns {#organize-political-campaigns}

To substantially change the laws of our home country, the United States, one way or another, it is not enough to endorse voting for establishment political parties, nor is it enough to endorse votes for existing third parties. People talk about sending a message with your vote, but often there are far more  and *better* opportunities to express a clearer message than “this old white guy is the lesser of two evils”. To get into office people that actually represent the views of the people, it will be absolutely necessary for there to be a coordinated political campaign that aims to capture the agreement of 100,000,000 people (1.25% of the world’s population, 30% of the United States population) to vote for the candidate chosen through bottom-up processes that elicit candidates who represent the expressed interests of the people. This can and should dovetail with the mission of getting people to agree on and use a grassroots candidate selection system, which is external to the government, yet designed at its core to be beholden to public interests rather than profit or any privileged individuals’ personal interests.

# **What role does the Moneyless Society nonprofit play?** {#what-role-does-the-moneyless-society-nonprofit-play?}

Two lists are below: one that describes what we are already doing, and another that describes things we recognize must happen but haven’t yet built the foundation/network to implement ourselves. In striving to fulfill our mission, we will gradually move items from the second list here (there and then) to the first (here and now). 

## Here and Now {#here-and-now}

**Podcast** to open people’s minds to new ways of thinking that are conducive to collective healing and decentralized efforts to create a commons that can benefit us all.  
Writing informative and thought provoking content on our website to entwine the blossoming spring of critical thinking and compassion.  
Development of **digital infrastructure**. Testing it as we go to learn the ropes of what we’ll end up doing later.  
Nurturing compassionate values within our shared spaces.  
**Networking** on behalf of people we know so they can connect with each other about shared passions or related project ideas. The sooner two people who are making the same app start talking, the more work they can eliminate and the more time they have.  
Collaborating on projects that benefit the commons or otherwise empower the disempowered (including ourselves), refining them along the way as we learn more about people’s needs. Examples include: 

* providing free meals  
* writing open source code  
* connecting those apps to each other  
* boosting each others’ fundraising efforts and social advocacy

Providing free community meals through the Appalachian Community Meal project, kickstarting grassroots efforts to provide people’s needs. 

## There and then {#there-and-then}

Using infrastructure for coordinated, diffuse action to accomplish projects of greater scale than local communities, or even global digital communities can manage alone.  
Fiscally sponsoring direct action projects  
Supporting development and maintenance of open source web applications   
Curating a library of open source knowledge for testing and replicating.  
Developing innovative technology and techniques for providing basic needs.
