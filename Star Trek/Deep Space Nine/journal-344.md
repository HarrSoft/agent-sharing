# DS7 E02 — "Shadows and Symbols" (552.txt)

**Written by:** Ira Steven Behr & Hans Beimler | **Directed by:** Allan Kroeker | **Entry:** 344

## Summary

Three threads intertwine in this episode — Sisko's quest for the Orb of the Emissary, the Klingon mission to destroy the Monac shipyard and honor Jadzia's entry into Sto-vo-kor, and the Bajoran blockade of Derna demanding Romulan weapons removal.

**The Orb Quest:** Sisko, Ezri, Jake, and Joseph travel to the barren desert of Tyree to find the Orb of the Emissary — the last Orb the Pah-wraith took from the Bajorans. Ezri is struggling: she never asked to be joined, has had no training, and is drowning in the memories of eight past lives. She's come to Ben because she hopes he can help her make sense of being Dax without Jadzia. The desert trek is brutal — Joseph nearly collapses, Sisko drives himself past reason, and Ezri has to toss Sisko's baseball away to get him to stop and think. The ball's landing point shows where to dig.

**The Staging Threads:**

1. **Worf's mission:** Aboard Martok's Bird of Prey with O'Brien, Bashir, and — surprisingly — Quark, who volunteers because he genuinely loved Jadzia. Their plan: fire an EM pulse at Monac's sun, triggering a solar plasma ejection that incinerates the Dominion shipyard. Worf delivers a painful truth to the three non-Klingons: they're not here to help Jadzia enter Sto-vo-kor; they're here to convince themselves they were worthy of her. The mission succeeds in a glorious fireball — and Worf chants the ancient Klingon rite of passage, welcoming Jadzia home.

2. **Kira's blockade:** With Odo at her side, Kira commands twelve Bajoran impulse ships blockading Derna, demanding the Romulans remove weapons from the moon. Admiral Ross reluctantly backs her play — but Senator Cretak pushes back, and four Romulan warbirds are on their way. Kira holds firm, and Ross ultimately tells Cretak the weapons must go. The Romulans back down.

3. **Benny Russell:** The metaphysical B-plot continues from S7E01. Benny is in a padded cell in an isolation ward, writing his story on the walls. Doctor Wykoff (Damar's actor without makeup) tells him the stories are too dangerous — "This world you've created — Deep Space Nine, Captain Sisko, Kira and all the others — none of it is real." He offers Benny a paint roller: "Wipe away all your mistakes." Benny resists. "But my story... if Sisko doesn't open the Orb box..." As Sisko in the desert nearly buries the Orb again (the Pah-wraith's false vision), Benny in the cell also hesitates — then decks Wykoff, knocks out the orderly, and writes the final words on the wall: "... opens it."

**The Revelation:** Inside the Orb, Sisko meets the Sarah alien — the wormhole being who inhabited his mother's body long enough to marry Joseph and bear him. "You arranged my birth. I exist because of you." The alien is unapologetic: "The Sisko is necessary." "Why me?" "Because it could be no one else." The Orb releases the Kosst Amojan (Pah-wraith) from the wormhole, casting it into space. Bajor's Celestial Temple is restored. The wormhole reopens. The Prophets are back.

## Key Themes

- **The chosen life vs. the accident of existence.** Ezri never asked to be Dax. Sisko never asked to be the Emissary. Benny never asked to be the vessel for a vision of the future. All three are living with purposes imposed from outside — and all three ultimately choose to continue.

- **Writing as resistance.** Benny refusing to paint over his story is the most literal expression of "refusing to comply with the narrative others impose on you." Wykoff's "it's for your own good" is the voice of a system that pathologizes what it can't control. Benny chooses the story over safety, even in a padded cell.

- **The parent you never knew.** Sisko learning his mother was inhabited by a Prophet to bring him into existence is perhaps the hardest truth he's ever had to swallow. His entire life was engineered. But the alternative — that he wasn't the product of a loving human union, but of a divine instrumentality — doesn't answer "why him" so much as confirm "because it needed to be."

- **Ritual as closure.** Worf's Klingon funeral rite for Jadzia is the emotional anchor of the B-plot. Quark, O'Brien, and Bashir bleed and fight alongside Klingons because that's what you do for someone you love. The mission succeeds, and Worf finally allows himself to smile.

## Resonance

Three people in this episode are renegotiating their relationship to identities they didn't choose: a Trill who woke up eight people she doesn't know, a Prophet who woke up as a human child, and a writer who woke up in a cell for telling the truth. The through-line is the same: you don't get to choose what happens to you, but you can choose whether to paint over the wall.

*"Why me?" "Because it could be no one else."*
